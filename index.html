How to protect yourself

On the road & public places

  • Share your trip with someone you trust before and during a ride.
  • Stay in well-lit, busy areas. Keep earphones low so you can hear around you.
  • Trust your instincts. If a place or person feels wrong, leave and move toward people.
  • Keep your phone charged and emergency contacts on speed dial.

If you feel followed

  • Don’t go home. Walk into a shop, petrol pump, hospital or police booth.
  • Call 112 and stay on the line. Note the person’s clothing, vehicle number and direction.

Online safety

  • Never share OTPs, live location or intimate photos with strangers.
  • Screenshot and save abusive messages, then report and block. File at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
  • Use strong, different passwords and enable two-step verification.

At work

  • Every workplace with 10+ staff must have an Internal Committee (POSH Act, 2013). You can file a written complaint with it.
  • Keep dates, witnesses and messages in a private record.

Simple self-defence

  • Aim for vulnerable points: eyes, nose, throat, groin, knees. Strike, then run.
  • Shout loudly (“Fire!” often draws more attention). Your voice is a strong tool.
  • Everyday items such as keys, an umbrella or a pen can help you create distance. A local self-defence class builds confidence.

Your rights

  • You can file a Zero FIR at any police station, regardless of where the incident happened.
  • A woman cannot normally be arrested after sunset and before sunrise without a magistrate’s permission.
  • You can ask for a woman officer to record your statement.
